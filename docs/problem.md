# The problem

## Land stuck in a dead owner's name

- Karnataka has roughly 45 to 52 lakh land records (RTCs) still in the names of owners who have died. Press reports give different numbers; the Revenue Minister has cited 48 lakh. ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/karnataka-has-29-lakh-lands-in-the-name-of-dead-people-3244098), [Hans India](https://www.thehansindia.com/karnataka/govt-launches-special-campaign-to-transfer-land-records-to-heirs-942796))
- "Pauti" (also written pouthi, pauthi) is the inheritance mutation: moving the dead owner's name in the RTC to the legal heirs.
- Until that happens, heirs can't get government schemes, bank loans or other benefits that need legal land ownership. (Hans India)

## Why families don't apply

The government runs a doorstep Pauti Khate campaign, but reported reasons for not applying are:

- fees
- family disputes and siblings who don't agree
- not knowing who the legal heirs are
- missing documents

The Revenue Minister has also said relatives deny women their share. ([Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/kin-denying-women-land-share-karnataka-minister-krishna-byre-gowda-3895511))

## The wider picture

- Record updates lag behind the records: PRS reports 86% of land records computerised but only 47% of mutation records, and 28% of records of rights digitally signed (2017). ([PRS](https://prsindia.org/policy/analytical-reports/land-records-and-titles-india))
- A 2026 Lok Sabha committee report notes that changes from inheritance, sale, partition and court orders are not reflected promptly. ([Lok Sabha](https://sansad.in/getFile/app/lsscommittee/Rural%20Development%20and%20Panchayati%20Raj/18_Rural_Development_and_Panchayati_Raj_41.pdf?source=app))
- Land disputes are often cited as about two-thirds of pending court cases (World Bank 2007, via PRS), taking about 20 years to resolve (NITI Aayog, via PRS).
- Counter-evidence we take seriously: a Delhi High Court study found record-related disputes are only 13.6% of property litigation there. So we don't claim to fix court backlogs; we fix one process. ([Carnegie](https://carnegieendowment.org/events/2021/09/land-litigation-in-india-learnings-from-a-study-on-land-litigation-in-delhi))

## Who is hit hardest

Families with low literacy and a low-cost phone, far from the taluk office, who can't pay an agent. Women heirs, whose share is easy to leave out.
