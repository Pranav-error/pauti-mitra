# How other countries handle it, and what we take from each

We read summaries and abstracts of these; check the full texts before quoting exact numbers.

| Country / approach | What they did | What happened | What we take |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rwanda**, Land Tenure Regularization | Registered about 8.4 million parcels in three years with a low-cost, participatory process ([World Bank](https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/d63797f6-e70f-54b9-8dd8-6ab36c658793/content), [IGC](https://theigc.org/collections/rwanda-land-tenure-regularisation)) | The pilot recorded inheritance rights without gender bias ([Ali, Deininger, Goldstein](https://wider.unu.edu/publication/environmental-and-gender-impacts-land-tenure-regularization-africa)). But later transfers mostly stayed unregistered; local offices and information campaigns reduced this, and didn't end it ([FAO AGRIS record](https://agris.fao.org/search/zh/records/67126e147f591113e2a68022)) | A one-time drive isn't enough. Records go stale unless updating stays cheap and local, which is the role Pauti Mitra plays after Karnataka's campaign |
| **Estonia**, notary-led succession | A notary identifies the heirs and issues a certificate of succession; the heir applies to the land register online with a digital signature; entering the heir costs no state fee ([EU e-Justice](https://e-justice.europa.eu/topics/family-matters-inheritance/inheritance/information-authorities-issuing-ecs/ee_en), [NJORD](https://www.njordlaw.com/what-keep-mind-when-inheriting-immovable-and-making-transactions-inherited-immovable-estonia)) | A short path: one certified heir decision, one online application | Do the heir decision once, in a form that matches the official family tree certificate, and reuse it for the application |
| **Ethiopia**, low-cost certification with joint titles | Cheap, community-led certification; certificates in both spouses' names ([Deininger, Ali, Holden, Zevenbergen 2008](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/ref/10.1080/00220388.2017.1327662), [Holden and Bezu](https://nmbu.brage.unit.no/nmbu-xmlui/bitstream/11250/2479338/1/clts_wp_6_14_rev1.pdf)) | Mixed but positive: some gains in women's say in decisions and empowerment ([Melesse, Dabissa, Bulte 2018](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/jdevst/v54y2018i10p1756-1774.html)) | Put every heir, including wives and daughters, on the record, not just one "head" |
| **Peru**, COFOPRI urban titling | Titles to over 1.2 million urban households, 1996 to 2003 | Titled households worked more outside the home and child labour fell ([Field, QJE 2007](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/771051)) | Evidence that a clear title changes how families live and work |
| **Tanzania**, MAST (USAID) | Villagers record land rights on a mobile app with trained local helpers ([USAID evaluation](https://pdf.usaid.gov/pdf_docs/PA00M7ZK.pdf), [cost study](https://pdf.usaid.gov/pdf_docs/PA00T8KT.pdf)) | Piloted, then scaled to about 40 more villages ([Land Links](https://land-links.org/?p=3122)) | Phone app plus a trained local person works; matches our operator mode |
| **Georgia**, blockchain registry | Hashes of land transactions anchored on a public blockchain from 2016 ([Eurasianet](https://eurasianet.org/georgia-authorities-use-blockchain-technology-for-developing-land-registry)) | Credit belongs mostly to earlier institutional reform; a blockchain doesn't fix bad input or update itself ([U4](https://www.u4.no/blog/anti-corruption-reforms-successful-in-georgia-blockchain-stealing-limelight), [New America](https://www.newamerica.org/insights/blockchain-for-property-rights-georgia/)) | No blockchain. Feed the official process with correct input instead |
| **Fit-for-purpose land administration** (FIG and World Bank, 2014) | Global guidance: land systems should be flexible, inclusive, participatory, affordable and upgradeable ([FIG Publication 60](https://fig.net/resources/publications/figpub/pub60/figpub60.asp)) | Promoted for national programmes, for example in Africa ([UNECA](https://archive.uneca.org/sites/default/files/uploaded-documents/LPI/CLPA_2017/Presentations/a_fit-for-purpose_approach_to_land_administration_in_africa.pdf)) | Our design rules: cheap, works on basic phones, assisted mode, upgradeable to other states |
| **Social Tenure Domain Model** (UN-Habitat GLTN) | Open-source, pro-poor land information tool on PostGIS and QGIS, based on the ISO 19152 land model ([GLTN](https://oldweb.unhabitat.org/sites/default/files/documents/Social%20tenure%20domain%20model.pdf), [OSGeo](https://wiki.osgeo.org/wiki/STDM)) | Used in pilots; no independent evaluation found | Shape our case file (people, parcels, rights) on the ISO 19152 model so it can connect to other systems |

## The best approach, putting it together

1. **One certified heir decision** (Estonia), produced by tested rules and matching the official family tree certificate.
2. **Every heir on the record**, women included (Ethiopia; Deininger et al. on Karnataka's 1994 reform).
3. **A cheap, local, ongoing channel** so records don't go stale after the campaign (Rwanda): voice on a phone, or a trained operator at a service centre (Tanzania MAST).
4. **Fit-for-purpose, not high-tech for its own sake** (FIG / World Bank; Georgia's lesson): no blockchain, just correct input into the existing government process.
5. **Standard data model** (ISO 19152 via STDM) so the case file can plug into other states' systems later.
